Settle it under the terms you agreed to.

When a customer and an online shop disagree about a return, or their agents do, the desk applies the returns terms the shop published on the day of purchase. Published rules compute the outcome, and both sides can recompute it to the same fingerprint. The desk runs in your browser and never receives your case.

How a case runs

  1. The receipt pins the terms

    At checkout the shop's receipt names its returns terms by version and SHA-256 fingerprint.

  2. The desk reads them from the shop

    With your OK, the desk fetches that version from the shop's own site and checks the fingerprint. Nothing about you is sent.

  3. Published rules decide

    A short published decision function computes the outcome from the terms, the receipt and your claim. Your assistant explains it and cannot change it.

  4. The shop's agent has its say

    The shop publishes its own agent and its offers. The desk loads that agent by URL and runs it in your browser, with your model.

  5. Both sides confirm

    You choose an option. The shop opens the case link on its own site, recomputes the fingerprint and confirms.

Why either side can check it

Terms from the day you bought

A later change to a shop's terms does not apply to an older order. The fingerprint on the receipt names the exact text that applies.

Same inputs, same fingerprint

The decision function is plain JavaScript with no clock, no network and no randomness. Anyone can run it on a case file and compare the result.

The desk never receives your case

This site is static. The case lives in your browser and in the links you and the shop send each other. Agents run in your browser with the model and key you choose.

Where the automation stops

  • The desk applies a shop's own published terms. It does not say what the law requires, gives no legal advice, and leaves any rights you have under the law untouched.
  • It is not a court, a public body or a dispute resolution entity. The two shops in this demo are fictional.
  • Every automated step is shown before it runs, and either side can ask for review by a person. In this demo no reviewer is on duty.
  • Facts the shop disputes, such as whether an item was used, are marked for review by a person, because the rules cannot decide who is right about them.

Why build this now

The European Union stopped taking complaints on its central online dispute resolution platform on 20 March 2025 and closed it on 20 July 2025. The European Parliament's summary of the repeal says its performance did not justify its public and private costs (procedure 2023/0375(COD)). The revised rules for consumer dispute bodies ask that automated means be disclosed in advance and that parties can request review by a natural person (procedure 2023/0376(COD), recital 21).

As agents start to shop and to handle returns for people and for shops, more of these disagreements will happen between agents. This demo shows a procedure that a small shop can adopt by publishing two files, that both sides can verify, and that holds no case data.

For shops and agents

A shop adopts the procedure by publishing its returns terms as versioned JSON, printing the version and fingerprint on each receipt, and adding a resolution block to its agent manifest. It can also publish a representative agent with its offers. Read the protocol.
